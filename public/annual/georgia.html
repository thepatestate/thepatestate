No. 01 · GeorgiaThe Autopsy

Start with the autopsy

Why 2025 ended · What the offseason answered

As Georgia prepares to open against Tennessee State, the 2025 record starts with three scores: a 24-21 home loss to Alabama on September 27, a 28-7 win over Alabama in the SEC Championship Game on December 6, and a 39-34 Sugar Bowl quarterfinal loss to Ole Miss on January 2. Georgia won the SEC, but 34 points weren't enough in its last game.

Georgia averaged 32.1 points, rushed for 2,553 yards and held the ball for 34 minutes per game, third nationally. It finished 55th nationally in plays of at least 20 yards and outside the top 50 in total offense for a second straight season. The Bulldogs had 18 fewer gains of at least 10 yards than in 2024, even while finishing fourth in the SEC in rushing.

Photo slotJanuary 2, New OrleansGame photo · 39-34 Sugar Bowl quarterfinal
Georgia scored 34 and went home. Ole Miss won 39-34 in the Sugar Bowl quarterfinal after Georgia held the ball without enough big plays.

Kirby Smart pointed to play-action: missed vision, busted routes and busted protection kept the run game from producing chunk throws. Gunner Stockton completed 69.7 percent of his passes, threw 24 touchdowns and five interceptions, and added 10 rushing scores. Against Ole Miss, Georgia scored 34 while its defense allowed 39.

Georgia allowed 17.6 points and 298.1 yards per game and gave up 1,142 rushing yards in 14 games. It also produced 19 sacks, seven interceptions and 13 forced turnovers. Georgia finished even in turnover margin after 13 giveaways.

Georgia returns 99.7 percent of its passing production and 99.3 percent of its rushing production. It also has to replace six of Stockton's top seven pass catchers.— The annual

No. 01 · GeorgiaThe Offense

The championship case

Stockton · The Bobo question · Tight-end answers · The fifth lineman

Georgia is No. 3 in both major preseason polls but No. 1 on my board, with 99.7 percent of its passing production, 99.3 percent of its rushing production and four experienced offensive-line building blocks back. The counterweight is six of the top seven pass catchers gone. Georgia has continuity everywhere except the place that must create more explosive plays.

Gunner Stockton completed 69.7 percent of his passes for 2,894 yards, 24 touchdowns and five interceptions in his first full season as the starter. He added 462 rushing yards and 10 touchdowns, finished seventh in the Heisman Trophy voting and won at Tennessee, Auburn, Florida and Alabama. His best stretches looked like a championship quarterback; his worst left Georgia without enough clean play-action explosives. Kirby Smart has cited missed vision, busted routes and busted protection. Stockton has to make the routine throws and reads more often before he has to create after the play breaks down.

Photo slotGunner StocktonGame photo · run action, eyes reset downfield
The year-two assignment. Stockton’s 69.7 percent accuracy and 34 total touchdowns need to produce more play-action gains from a 2,553-yard rushing offense.

Georgia must replace six of its top seven pass catchers without importing a Zachariah Branch-level target. Branch had 81 catches for 811 yards and six touchdowns, while Oscar Delp, Colbie Young, Dillon Bell and Noah Thomas combined for 89 catches, 1,141 yards and eight scores. London Humphreys averaged 15.3 yards on 18 receptions, and Georgia Tech transfer Isiah Canion caught 33 passes last season. Humphreys and Canion need to win outside, with Talyn Taylor and the younger receivers making defenses pay for crowding the run game. Arkansas on September 19 is the first hard look.

Mike Bobo enters his fourth year as coordinator after Georgia scored 32.1 points per game, rushed for 2,553 yards and held the ball for 34 minutes per game in 2025. It also finished 55th nationally in plays of 20-plus yards and had 18 fewer gains of at least 10 yards than the year before. Smart has defended Bobo’s production and team success. The line and backfield are experienced enough that the call sheet needs to produce more play-action gains.

Nate Frazier ran for 947 yards and six touchdowns at 5.5 per carry, and Chauncey Bowens added 526 yards and six scores at 5.1. Their 1,473 yards on 276 carries give Georgia a sound starting point. Dwight Phillips Jr. added 210 yards at 6.0 per carry, while Kentucky transfer Dante Dowdell entered fall camp with an uncertain status after serious injuries in an ATV accident. Georgia can spread the early carries without running Frazier down, then use the run game to pull linebackers downhill and clear throwing lanes for Stockton.

The tight ends can carry more of the passing game while the receiver order settles. Lawson Luckie had 15 catches for 158 yards and four touchdowns and takes over the every-down role vacated by Delp. Elyiss Williams averaged 16.7 yards on seven receptions; Jaden Reddell and Ethan Barbour give Georgia more options, though Barbour is returning from the significant leg injury that ended his 2025 season early. Earnest Greene III, Drew Bobo, Dontrell Glover and Juan Gaston form the experienced line base, with Greene and Bobo carrying recent injury histories. Zykie Helton and Daniel Calhoun are competing at guard, Michael Uini remains in the mix, Jahzare Jackson can play tackle, and Gaston can play right guard or right tackle.

The most important position battle

The fifth offensive lineman · and where Juan Gaston plays
Photo slotJuan Gaston and the right sideCamp photo · one player, two possible positions

Georgia has four experienced building blocks, but the fifth selection sets two jobs. If Helton or Calhoun wins right guard, Gaston can stay at right tackle. If Uini or Jackson earns the tackle job, Gaston can move inside. With Bobo and Greene carrying recent injury histories, Georgia needs to settle on five instead of moving bodies every week. Stockton needs enough protection to reset after play-action.

If the fifth man holds

Helton or Calhoun secures guard, or Uini or Jackson secures tackle, and Gaston stays where the best five fit. Georgia can keep its combination blocks and protection timing intact.

If it doesn’t

Gaston becomes the weekly repair tool, the right side keeps changing and Bobo calls around protection instead of using the full field.

How you’ll know

Check Gaston’s position against Tennessee State on September 5, then see whether the same five open at Arkansas on September 19. A reshuffle means the job is still open.

No. 01 · GeorgiaThe Defense

Continuity, then rush

The defense · What left, what returns, the pressure clock

Heading into the opener, Georgia returns 16 of the 21 defenders who logged at least 150 snaps last season. It allowed 17.6 points per game but had 19 sacks in 14 games, while the two offenses that hit it hardest scored 24 and 39.

Defensive tackle Christen Miller, linebacker C.J. Allen and corner Daylen Everette became top-85 draft picks. Allen led Georgia with 88 tackles and 3.5 sacks, Everette had eight pass breakups and an interception return for a touchdown, and Miller supplied interior weight. The portal also took cornerbacks Dominick Kelly and Daniel Harris, safety Joenel Aguero, edge Elo Modozie, Jaden Harris and Adrian Maddox.

Pate State original artThe pressure pointSanford Stadium · A veteran defense searching for sacks
Nineteen sacks in 14 games. Georgia returns 16 of 21 defenders who played at least 150 snaps. The front has to turn 58 tackles for loss into more sacks.

The front returns Xzavier McLeod and Elijah Griffin. Raylen Wilson had 74 tackles, Chris Cole had 61 tackles and a team-high 4.5 sacks, and Justin Williams had 35 tackles and four tackles for loss. KJ Bolden had 77 tackles, while Ellis Robinson IV intercepted four passes. The open safety job is beside Bolden, with Clemson transfer Khalil Barnes in the mix. Georgia also added Amaris Williams from Auburn.

Georgia allowed 1,142 rushing yards in 14 games, about 81.6 per Saturday, but managed 1.36 sacks per game. It had 58 tackles for loss, seven interceptions and 13 forced turnovers. Alabama scored 24 in the September loss, then seven in the SEC Championship Game. Ole Miss scored 35 in October and 39 in the Sugar Bowl.

The Championship Ceiling sits with the edge room. Quintavius Johnson had two sacks last season, and Chase Linton had none in limited action. New outside linebackers coach Larry Knight inherits that group, with Amaris Williams as another option and McLeod and Griffin inside. Georgia needs an edge winner, pressure without extra rushers and a safety beside Bolden who can handle the coverage when help goes elsewhere. Ole Miss, which scored 39 to end Georgia’s season, is the Nov. 7 stop.

How opponents will attack Georgia

4 blueprints · and when each expires

11. Load the box and test the receivers

Single-high structure→The extra fitter downhill

Frazier, Bowens and Stockton had 1,935 rushing yards in 2025, while 6 of the top 7 pass catchers left. Defenses can live with isolated throws until Humphreys, Canion or a young receiver wins outside.

22. Change the play-action picture

A safe 4-man rush→A late robber or simulated pressure

Georgia finished 4th in the SEC in rushing but lost 18 plays of 10-plus yards from the previous season. Smart pointed to vision, routes and protection on missed play-action chances; opponents will cloud Stockton's first read.

33. Cage Stockton

Light edges and interior control→A scrape player after the mesh

Stockton turned 129 carries into 462 yards and 10 touchdowns. Keep him inside and make the rebuilt receiving corps win third down.

44. Tempo the safety-Star decision

Bunch, motion and trips→Switch releases into the conflict defender

Bolden is fixed, while the other safety spot and Star are tied to Barnes' placement. Fast offenses will force the secondary to declare its checks and find the defender still settling in.

What breaks the blueprintHumphreys or Canion winning outside breaks the loaded-box plan. Clean play-action protection restores the explosives Georgia missed in 2025. Johnson and Linton can create pressure with 4, freeing the linebackers. A settled Barnes alignment cuts down tempo's value. Oklahoma on Sept. 26 is the first check; Alabama on Oct. 10 is the bigger one.

Five things that are actually true

No propaganda department

Georgia returns the quarterback and run game.

Georgia returns 99.7 percent of its passing production and 99.3 percent of its rushing production, against 34.7 percent of its receiving production. Stockton, Nate Frazier and Chauncey Bowens stabilize the offense; the new target order has to carry Georgia to a championship.

The receiver issue is explosive plays.

Georgia has London Humphreys, Isiah Canion, Talyn Taylor, Lawson Luckie and a deep tight end room, but no returning 81-catch target after Zachariah Branch left. Georgia ranked 55th nationally in plays of 20-plus yards and produced 18 fewer plays of at least 10 yards despite rising to fourth in the SEC in rushing.

The schedule gives the new group 3 weeks.

Tennessee State, Western Kentucky and Arkansas precede Oklahoma on Sept. 26. By the fourth game, the receiver rotation and play-action protection must be settled enough for Oklahoma’s 14 returning starters.

The defense has a pass-rush bill due.

Georgia allowed 17.6 points and 1,142 rushing yards across 14 games, with Raylen Wilson, Chris Cole, KJ Bolden and Ellis Robinson IV back. It also recorded 19 sacks and 7 interceptions, which leaves too many passing downs on coverage.

The bye helps the 2 hardest weeks.

Georgia’s only open week falls on Oct. 24, after Alabama and Auburn and before Florida and Ole Miss. At 6–1, Georgia can use it to repair the offensive line and receiver rotation before the schedule’s 9.1/10 stress stretch.

What goes wrong

Georgia is No. 1 on my board, while the Pate State Model has Ohio State No. 1 and identifies the Buckeyes as the most likely champion. The model has its argument; Georgia returns a steadier group at quarterback, running back and across an experienced defense, with time for the unsettled pieces.

On Sept. 26, Georgia’s bet on its target group gets tested by Oklahoma. Isiah Canion joins London Humphreys, Talyn Taylor, the young receivers and a deep tight end room after Zachariah Branch left. At least 2 targets must stretch defenses, and play-action protection has to hold.

The 2025 defense allowed 17.6 points per game and 1,142 rushing yards, but it had 19 sacks and 7 interceptions. The Ole Miss loss made the cost clear: Georgia scored 34 and went home. The pass rush has to change elimination-game downs.

On Oct. 10, Georgia loses at Alabama, uses the Oct. 24 bye and wins at Ole Miss on Nov. 7. That puts Georgia at 11–1, with another SEC championship and the No. 1 playoff seed. Its 34-minute possession profile gives it a way to manage hard Saturdays while the passing game develops.

Georgia wins the national championship, 31–28 over Ohio State. The pick fails if 34.7 percent returning receiving production remains a problem, offensive-line health blocks play-action, or a 19-sack defense still needs blitzes in December. Georgia has the quarterback, run game, defensive experience and schedule shape for this pick. — JP
