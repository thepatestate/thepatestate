**October 3 at Clemson Is Where My Miami Take Gets Its First Real Answer**

*I said Miami is the best team in the ACC by far and everyone else is playing for second. The one named alternative Miami actually has a dated appointment with is 7-6 Clemson — and my own preseason bracket has Clemson ranked eight spots above the Hurricanes.*

Put the rhetoric down for a second and look at the calendar. Saturday, October 3, Miami goes to Clemson. That is the first date on Miami's schedule where a broad opinion about the ACC has to become a result, and it happens to involve one of the three teams I named out loud as the only realistic alternatives to Miami.

That is not a coincidence I get to hide from. I said Miami is the best team by far in the ACC and everybody else is playing for second, and I said if not them, then who — then I listed SMU, Louisville and Clemson as the names I couldn't make work. Two of those three do not appear on Miami's 2026 schedule. Clemson does, in Death Valley, in the first week of October.

[PULLQUOTE]

Be clear about what the claim is, because it is not the modest version. I'm not arguing Miami is a defensible pick to win the league. I'm arguing separation. And I attached exactly one condition to it: the starting quarterback has to be available. There is a massive drop-off from QB1 to QB2 in Coral Gables, and if that changes, the whole case changes with it. That is a caveat, not the argument.

## The paper case is easy

Miami went 13-3 last season and 6-2 in the ACC, and enters the year No. 7 in both the AP and the Coaches poll. Clemson went 7-6, finished 4-4 in the league, sits No. 23 in the Coaches poll and is unranked in the AP. If all you do is line those two profiles up, October 3 looks like a game Miami is supposed to win, and Miami is right to be favored.

So here's the part that complicates my own comfort. In my preseason bracket, Clemson is ranked No. 3 in the country. Miami is No. 11. Those are my numbers, published in August, and they say something noticeably different from what I said into a microphone about Miami's edge over Clemson specifically. The 7-6 is what Clemson did last fall. The No. 3 is what I think this roster is capable of doing this fall. Both belong to me.

## What a Clemson fan says back, and what a Miami fan says back

The Clemson objection is the easy one to state: my own board has them ahead of the team I keep calling the runaway ACC favorite, so why exactly am I waving them off as an alternative? Fair. I don't have a tidy answer beyond the fact that a national ranking and a conference favorite tag are answering slightly different questions, and that Clemson's finished 2025 record is the only thing either of us can actually point to.

The Miami objection is sharper, and it's the one I'd expect from a serious Hurricanes fan. Bracket projections are opinions. Records are events. Clemson went 7-6 and 4-4 and missed the AP poll entirely, and if Miami goes to Clemson and wins, the honest reading is that a top-ten team beat an unranked team on the road. That is not a referendum on the conference. That's Saturday working the way it's supposed to work.

I'd accept most of that. Where I'd push is on why the contradiction makes the game more interesting rather than less. I am effectively betting that a roster I personally rate third in the country will not show up in the standings against Miami. If Miami wins in that building with its starter under center, it beat a team I think is genuinely dangerous, not a team I quietly believe is bad. That's better evidence for my take than a comfortable win over somebody I never rated.

And Miami's own history is why the evidence has to be collected at all. We are still waiting on Miami to win the ACC. They have never won the conference title game. They were the overwhelming favorite last year and did not get it done. "Clear favorite" is a label that has not converted in Coral Gables recently, which is the whole reason I'd rather point at a date than keep repeating a sentence.

## What October 3 cannot do

Here is the limit, named honestly. A win at Clemson answers the challenge I picked, against the team on the schedule. It does not answer SMU. SMU matched Miami's 6-2 conference record last season, went 9-4, and enters this year No. 19 in the AP and No. 20 in the Coaches poll. That is a stronger public résumé than Clemson's 7-6 and unranked, and it is the one I named without ever engaging it on football terms. The dated meeting in evidence is Clemson. So Miami can win my argument exactly as I posed it and still never play the team with the best competing case.

Which is why I'd separate October 3 from the game everyone will circle instead. Miami at Notre Dame on November 7 at 7:30 will tell you plenty about Miami's ceiling. It will tell you nothing about the ACC.

## The test

Saturday, October 3, two things are observable. Whether Miami's starting quarterback is available, and whether Miami wins in Clemson. With the starter in place, a road win is the cleanest early evidence that the gap I described is real — and it counts for more, not less, because of how highly I rated that opponent in my own bracket. A Clemson win hands my rhetorical question a specific, immediate answer, and I'd have to say so plainly.

October 3 won't crown an ACC champion, and it won't tell you whether Miami has cleared SMU, because SMU isn't on this schedule. What it will do is put a scoreboard behind two of my numbers that currently disagree with each other. One of them is about to get tested, and not by me. If you think Clemson's 7-6 makes that a formality, mark the date and let's compare notes on the sixth.

It is the head-to-head result that settles any tiebreaker between the league's two most talented rosters. It is Miami's lone trip to a ranked conference opponent, in a venue that has ended more favorite runs than any other stop in this league. And it is the one Saturday all season when the rest of the ACC gets to find out, in real time, whether the twelve-spot gap is a fact or a projection. Win in Death Valley and the conversation about the ACC race is functionally over in the first week of October.

The proving window comes early instead. Miami takes three road trips before that Clemson date settles anything: Stanford on September 4, Wake Forest on September 18, then the trip to Death Valley. Everyone else in this league is waiting on somebody above them to slip.

        
If the Mustangs turn in both, the "then who" question gets a real answer, and I'll write that column too.
