No. 03 · ClemsonThe Autopsy

Start with the autopsy

Why 2025 ended · What the offseason answered

As Clemson prepares to open at LSU, the Tigers carry a 7-6 record and six losses that exposed more than one problem. Clemson averaged 27.2 points overall but 21.8 in those losses, with a run game, third-down offense and secondary that didn't hold together when the schedule tightened.

Clemson entered 2025 ranked No. 4 and lost 17-10 to LSU on Aug. 30. Georgia Tech followed with a 24-21 loss on Sept. 13, then Syracuse won 34-21 in Death Valley on Sept. 20. The Tigers recovered long enough to create hope before SMU won 35-24 on Oct. 18. Duke's 46-45 win on Nov. 1 dropped Clemson to 3-5, and Penn State closed the season with a 22-10 Pinstripe Bowl win on Dec. 27. Clemson finished 7-6, its worst record since 2010.

Photo slotNovember 1, Death ValleyGame photo · Duke 46, Clemson 45
The night Clemson fell to 3-5. Clemson scored 45 points and still lost after a fourth-down coverage failure helped Duke build a 28-point first half. The staff overhaul and secondary changes began with nights like this one.

The offense averaged 27.2 points, but that fell to 21.8 across the six losses. Clemson rushed for 124.5 yards per game, No. 105 nationally, and converted 60 of 175 third downs, 34.3 percent and No. 113 nationally. Cade Klubnik completed 65.6 percent for 2,943 yards, but Clemson scored 10 against LSU and 10 against Penn State. The opener and the bowl ended with the same problem.

The defense produced 34 sacks and 85 tackles for loss while allowing 20.5 points per game. Sammy Brown had 107 tackles, 14 tackles for loss and 5.5 sacks; Will Heldt added 14.5 tackles for loss and a team-high six sacks. Behind them, Clemson allowed 251.2 passing yards per game, its most in 10 seasons and 11th in the ACC, with seven team interceptions. Opponents averaged 29.7 points in Clemson's losses. Duke's 46 made the point: disruption up front didn't produce enough reliable coverage behind it.

The six losses didn't come from one problem. Clemson had to fix the run game, third down and the back end at the same time.— Josh Pate

Nine Clemson players were drafted, including Blake Miller and Peter Woods in Round 1, T.J. Parker and Avieon Terrell in Round 2, and Klubnik in Round 4. Seven defensive starters left for the NFL or the portal, while 15 players entered the portal, including Ricardo Jones, Khalil Barnes and Stephiylan Green. Clemson added 11 transfers, nine on defense, and is expected to carry seven portal players on the defensive two-deep.

Brown and Heldt return in the front seven. Ashton Hampton started every game at corner, while T.J. Moore and Bryant Wesco Jr. combined for 83 catches, 1,374 yards and 10 touchdowns. Clemson has high-end ability, but less continuity than it had a year ago.

No. 03 · ClemsonThe Offense

The championship case

Vizzina · The Morris reset · Vertical stress · The center hinge

Clemson passed on a transfer quarterback despite having one combined start in the room, placing a 105-attempt bet on Christopher Vizzina. He has three years of preparation, Chad Morris has a new offense to install, and Harris Sewell has a move to center before LSU on Sept. 5. The opener is set for Sept. 5.

Vizzina completed 45 of 71 passes for 406 yards, four touchdowns and one interception in 2025. His one start brought 317 yards, three touchdowns and a 69% completion rate in a 35-24 loss to SMU, after Clemson scored seven first-half points. His career sample remains 105 attempts. Morris can call the full offense with Vizzina's arm and mobility, but Vizzina must handle pressure, work with a new center and keep Clemson on schedule at LSU.

Photo slotChristopher VizzinaGame photo · Eyes downfield, pocket closing
The roster's biggest bet. Clemson passed on a transfer quarterback and left the competition to a fourth-year player with 105 career attempts and a true freshman. Vizzina went 45-of-71 for 406 yards, four touchdowns and one interception in 2025.

True freshman Tait Reynolds went 7-of-10 for 74 yards and a rushing touchdown in the spring game. He also took five sacks and had assignment errors on a read option, motion and a throw's timing. Morris said Reynolds has the arm strength and skill set, but did not play his senior year. Starting him in Baton Rouge would put more strain on Clemson's communication. Vizzina needs to keep Reynolds on the sideline.

Morris returns for his second stint as Clemson's offensive coordinator after his 2011-14 run, replacing Garrett Riley. Clemson averaged 124.5 rushing yards and converted 60 of 175 third downs last season. Morris wants the run game and tempo to give Vizzina fewer obvious passing downs and create vertical throws before defenses can substitute.

T.J. Moore caught 52 passes for 837 yards and four touchdowns. Bryant Wesco Jr. had 31 catches for 537 yards and six scores; their 16.1 and 17.3 yards per catch give Clemson downfield targets. Olsen Patt-Henry returns after 16 catches for 126 yards, and 71.2% of Clemson's receiving production returns. Gideon Davidson had 60 carries for 260 yards as a freshman and joins SMU transfer Chris Johnson Jr. Davidson had no rushing touchdowns, and Clemson's returning rushing-production share is minus-37.7%.

Clemson allowed 21 sacks in 13 games, but three of last year's five primary line starters must be replaced. At least four roles change as Sewell moves from guard to center. He has 17 starts in 35 games and has not played center in college. Sewell must set protections for an inexperienced quarterback while Clemson uses tempo. If the line cannot create a run threat, LSU can rush the passer on obvious downs.

The most important position battle

Photo slotHarris SewellCamp photo · Making the calls from center

If it holds

Sewell handles exchanges and protection IDs, the interior gives Morris room to use tempo, and Davidson and Johnson keep Clemson out of third-and-long. Vizzina gets vertical chances instead of rescue assignments.

If it doesn't

At LSU on Sept. 5, chart clean exchanges, free interior rushers, pre-snap resets and Clemson's third-down distance. Six or more yards on repeated third downs means the offense is behind schedule.

No. 03 · ClemsonThe Defense

The defense has a new back end and six days

What left · what returns · what arrived · the communication clock

Clemson allowed 20.5 points per game and produced 34 sacks, then lost seven defensive starters to the draft or portal. The front returns production.

Clemson lost five defenders in the 2026 NFL Draft — Peter Woods, T.J. Parker, Avieon Terrell, Wade Woodaz and DeMonte Capehart — from a nine-player draft class. The portal also took Ricardo Jones, Khalil Barnes and Stephiylan Green, among others, leaving seven 2025 defensive starters gone through the draft or transfer market. That includes the best corner and Jones, who had six interceptions and Clemson’s only interception-return touchdown. Clemson allowed 251.2 passing yards per game, its most in 10 seasons.

The disruption returns. The coverage has to change. Sammy Brown and Will Heldt combined for 28.5 tackles for loss and 11.5 sacks in 2025. Behind them, Clemson is replacing seven defensive starters through a nine-man defensive portal class and a reorganized passing-game staff.

Sammy Brown and Will Heldt return 28.5 tackles for loss and 11.5 of Clemson’s 34 sacks. Brown led Clemson with 107 tackles, 14 tackles for loss and 5.5 sacks; Heldt had 14.5 tackles for loss and a team-high six sacks. Ashton Hampton started every game and recorded 45 tackles, eight passes defended and an interception. He takes the top-corner assignment after Terrell’s departure. Ronan Hanafin returns, while Kobe McCloud is expected to replace Woodaz after three career starts.

Clemson had seven interceptions and gave up 34 points to Syracuse, 35 to SMU and 46 to Duke in three conference losses.

The response was nine defensive portal additions, all rated three-stars in the supplied portal data. Elliot Washington II, London Merritt, Corey Myrick and Jerome Carter III are expected to receive immediate work. Donovan Starr, C.J. Wesley, Devarrick Woods, Markus Strong and Kourtney Kelly add competition across three levels. Seven portal defenders are projected in the two-deep. Thomas Allen is the defensive passing game coordinator, with Nolan Turner coaching the safeties. The back five has to get the same call before LSU arrives Sept. 5.

Five things that are actually true

No nostalgia, no panic, no pretending the machine agrees

The boldest bet is the quarterback Clemson did not take

One career start belongs to Clemson’s quarterbacks combined after Dabo Swinney declined to pursue a transfer. Christopher Vizzina begins in pole position after completing 69 percent for 317 yards and three touchdowns against SMU, though Clemson scored seven first-half points. His pressure clock and the rebuilt line’s communication face LSU on Sept. 5.

The receiving room is the cleanest playoff argument

Moore and Wesco combined for 83 catches, 1,374 yards and 10 touchdowns in 2025. Clemson returns 71.2 percent of its receiving production against a 14.1 percent passing share, giving the new quarterback established answers.

The defensive backbone survived the draft exodus

Brown and Heldt combined for 11.5 sacks and 28.5 tackles for loss last season. Clemson replaces seven starters through the NFL Draft or portal, and nine Clemson players were drafted, including three defenders in the first two rounds.

The secondary problem was structural

Clemson allowed 251.2 passing yards per game in 2025, its highest figure in 10 seasons and 11th in the ACC. Avieon Terrell left as a second-round pick, Ricardo Jones transferred after six interceptions, and Ashton Hampton, new back-end coaching roles and portal additions must communicate quickly.

My Clemson projection disagrees with the model

The Pate State Model ranks Clemson No. 20, while my bracket places Clemson No. 3, a 17-spot gap. The Coaches Poll puts Clemson No. 23. I’m betting on the ACC path, the receiving corps and the staff overhaul against 41.4 percent overall returning production.

What goes wrong

At 10–2, Clemson wins the ACC and takes the No. 3 seed in my bracket. The Coaches Poll has Clemson No. 23, the machine has Clemson No. 20, and my No. 3 projection carries a 17-spot gap.

Moore and Wesco produced 1,374 receiving yards last season. Davidson and Johnson give Chad Morris backfield options; if Vizzina distributes on time and Sewell runs the front at center, Clemson needs better than last season’s 124.5 rushing yards per game and functional pressure-down answers.

Nine draft picks left Clemson, which returns 41.4 percent of its overall production and replaces three primary offensive-line starters. The defense allowed its highest passing-yards-per-game figure in 10 years, then lost Terrell, Jones, Woodaz, Woods, Parker and Capehart. Brown and Heldt give Tom Allen a spine, but the portal additions must become a unit.

A 7-6 season forced staff changes, and the model’s skepticism is rational with 14.1 percent of passing production returning and rushing production reported at minus-37.7 percent. I see a receiving corps that can carry a new quarterback and an ACC schedule Clemson can control by beating Miami at home.

On Oct. 31 at Florida State, pressure on Vizzina, Sewell’s move to center, the run game and the secondary can break this projection. LSU is the first receipt, Miami is the largest, and the flag stays at 10–2. — JP
