2026 Season: Miami Owns the ACC, Oregon Can Break Through, and Michigan Made the Right Hire

Last season's results are being treated like permanent evidence, and that is where the public is getting this wrong. Miami's quarterback depth, Oregon's playoff history, and Michigan's unusual coaching search all point to a less settled season than the loudest predictions suggest.

The Pate State Staff · 785 words · Episode · Why EVERYONE Is Wrong About The 2026 Season - Josh Pate's College Football Show · Series · poll-day

The popular predictions around the 2026 season are turning last year's results into permanent evidence. I don't buy that. Miami is still the best team by far in the ACC, Oregon can eventually win the playoff games people say it cannot, and Michigan did about as well as it could have in hiring Kyle Whittingham.

Start with Miami, because this is the call I am most comfortable making. Darien Mensah is the piece that separates the Hurricanes from the rest of the league. If he is healthy and the roster remains largely what it appears to be on paper, I do not see SMU, Louisville, Clemson, or another ACC team taking the league from Miami. That is an aggressive opinion after Miami failed to win the conference last year while carrying overwhelming expectations. It is still my opinion.

Miami is the best team by far in the ACC, and everyone's playing for second.

The part that keeps this from being a casual preseason coronation sits behind Mensah. Miami has a massive drop from QB1 to QB2, and nobody has a model that can forecast an injury to the starter. If Mensah is unavailable, the ACC picture changes immediately. With him available, I would have to buy into SMU, Louisville, Clemson, or somebody else before I could move off Miami. I can't do that yet.

Oregon gets judged by a different kind of shortcut. The Ducks have been beaten in the Playoff, so the public has decided that choking is part of the program's identity. Oregon won the first playoff game ever, then shut out Texas Tech 23-0 last season before being beaten by the eventual national champion. Those losses happened. Dan Lanning's teams have been splattered before. That still does not prove Oregon cannot be the team doing the splattering one day.

I tend to believe Oregon will eventually break through. Maybe it happens this season, and maybe it does not. The point is that a few losses to teams that went on to win the national title do not create a law of nature. Oregon has not finished the job yet. That is different from being incapable of finishing it.

Indiana shows how quickly the measuring stick can move. If the Hoosiers went 8-4 after losing to Ohio State, Michigan, and USC, with Washington also in the mix, that would have been a phenomenal season for Indiana football until the last two years reset the standard. I can see the temperature around Curt Cignetti cooling if the team falls back, but I am not predicting that fall. Winning the national title was already a wild ascension. Nothing that comes after it would be crazier than getting there in the first place.

Texas carries the most dangerous expectation in the sport. If the Longhorns go 9-3 and miss the Playoff, or go 10-2 and get bounced early, the reaction around Steve Sarkisian will be unlike anything college football has handled before. A national title favorite gets judged by a different standard. That does not mean Texas is going to collapse. It means the expectation has become its own pressure point, and even a strong regular season could be treated as a failure if the postseason ends too soon.

Michigan deserves a pause before anybody grades the Whittingham hire. This was not a routine search after a coach was fired in the middle of a season for poor performance. The circumstances around the previous Michigan coach were unusual, and the instability at athletic director was already part of the market. I think Michigan did about as good as it could do given the circumstances.

I think they did about as good as they could do given the circumstances.

That does not promise a comfortable season in Ann Arbor. Bryce Underwood could show no improvement, and Michigan could finish 8-4. Those results would make the decision look worse later. They would not make the August decision unreasonable unless somebody can identify the obvious better hire Michigan passed over. The names offered so far do not make that case for me.

When the season starts, I am watching Mensah's health before I watch the ACC standings. I am watching Oregon's next playoff appearance for evidence that the old label has finally run out of usefulness. And I am watching Bryce Underwood's first stretch at Michigan, because his development will tell us more than an August argument about an unusual coaching search.

The 2026 season is not required to repeat the story people wrote about these programs last year. Miami can own the ACC, Oregon can finally break through, and Michigan can have made the right hire even if the record makes that decision uncomfortable to defend later.
