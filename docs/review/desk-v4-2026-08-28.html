Desk v4: the craft rewrite

What changed in how The Pate State writes, why the old copy read like a machine, and the same engine's output before and after — August 28, 2026.

What I read

Twenty-eight pieces fetched, sixteen read whole: AP news, game and feature stories; CBS Sports reporting (Zenitz, Marcello); Pat Forde's columns at SI; Ross Dellenger's reported features at Yahoo; Nicole Auerbach's Big Ten preview at NBC; Dennis Dodd's farewell; The Ringer's season primer. The notes live in the repo as a Claude Code skill, .claude/skills/sports-desk-editor/, with the fifteen moves those writers make (attributed excerpts), the twenty machine tells found in our own live copy, and a review format. npx tsx scripts/desk-review.mts <slug> prints any live piece with its metrics; --model adds a model-run review on the same rubric.

Why the copy read like a machine

It was structural, not cosmetic. Four things in the pipeline produced the register you hated:

  1. The writer never saw the story. It wrote from a JSON "reporting pack" — atomized facts, one-line quotes, numbers — never the source text and never the date. That is why every lead was a statistic, every quote was a one-line proof, and nothing said "Tuesday." The AP lead carries what happened, when, and what it pushes against in one sentence; ours could not, because the writer did not know when it was.
  2. The prompts were thirty prohibitions. A model writing under "do not, do not, do not" produces the cautious, symmetrical, hedged register — 2–3 sentences per paragraph, 18–24 words per sentence, "if A… if not…" pairs, "will show whether" endings. Journalists work from moves, not bans.
  3. The subtraction editor compressed everything into fact-bricks. Cutting 20–40% after the draft removed the rhythm along with the padding; the corpus mixes one-sentence paragraphs with five-sentence ones.
  4. The schema asked for the headline and dek before the body. The model filled in a form; the body came last.

So take the three teams I floated as hypotheticals and look at them. Clemson, the program I said was in the mix last year, went 7-6 and 4-4 in the ACC in 2025. It is unranked in the AP poll and No. 23 in the Coaches poll. Not that Miami is guaranteed, but after you write down the records and the rankings, nobody has handed me a better name.
