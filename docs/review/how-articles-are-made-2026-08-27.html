3 · What the gates and judges actually check

Code gates (fail closed, no model involved)

  • Banned language — ~40 regexes from the Voice Bible: consulting nouns, AI transitions, announced scaffolding ("the mechanism is"), coaching clichés, recruiting hype, announced candor, "the machine," §2.8 tells (delve, crucial, landscape, navigate), exemplar parroting.
  • Restatement — a sentence sharing 4+ content words with an earlier one is a restatement; over 12% of sentences = the piece "circles" and goes back.
  • Abstract paragraphs — 35+ words with no number and no proper noun; two of them fail the draft.
  • Hammer budget — isolated one-liners (≤12 words alone) capped at one per 400 words.
  • Word floors — 600 Wire / reaction, 800 columns.
  • Lane person — Josh's Read must have first person; the Wire must not.
  • Verbatim — any quoted span of 5+ words must be a substring of the transcript or the supplied quote.
  • Sourcing hygiene — no outlet names in headlines or prose, no "the report does not say," no attribution openers.
  • Thin source — under 2,200 chars of grounding, no Wire story at all.

AI judges (Sonnet, OpenAI fallback; fail open)

  • Fact-check — SOURCES vs DRAFT; verdict pass / unsupported / contradicted. Not a pass = the piece never publishes. hard stop
  • scoreDraft — 12 categories 1–10: voice, originality, specificity, evidence, pacing, personality, structural variety, value added, headline, accuracy, humanity (written vs generated), discovery. Below 8 in two categories = one rewrite with the notes. pass: <2 categories under 8
  • voiceMatch — reads the draft next to the lane's gold-standard build and scores register only. pass ≥ 8 · up to 2 rewrites
  • fanScore — a fan, not an editor: legibility, enjoyment, Josh-voice. Used by the review tooling to measure, not wired into production as a gate. target 8.5 · production pieces land ~6–7
  • Callout relevance — Josh's receipt only prints when a model confirms it's the same specific subject.

4 · Why this shape tops out around 7 on the fan judge

  1. One writer, one shot, then patch. Luna gets the whole kit and the whole transcript and writes the piece in a single pass; every later step can only send notes back ("fix these") to the same writer. There is no argument-planning step, no second draft to compare against, no editor pass — the judges are critics, not collaborators.
  2. The judges' notes don't move the writer. Rewrite rounds with "the same point restated four ways" as feedback produce a draft that restates it three ways. The rewrite is adopted only if it re-passes the gates, so most rounds end with the original.
  3. Floors manufacture the restating. A 600/800-word floor on a source that carries 300 words of real football gets filled with the same facts in new clothes; the restatement gate then bounces it, and the retry pads differently.
  4. Source shape sets the ceiling. Argument episodes (one take, football reasons) reach 7–7.5; list episodes and award-notes sources read as "a listicle wearing a monologue costume" at 5–6, regardless of voice instructions.
  5. The gold standard was made interactively. Josh's 9.7 column came from a draft plus Josh's own edits; the pipeline reproduces the register (voiceMatch passes) but not the judgment about what to leave out.
