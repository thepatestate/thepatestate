Texas Tech Keeps No. 1 2027 Recruit Jalen Brewster

The five-star defensive lineman shut down his recruitment and will remain committed to the Red Raiders over LSU and other contenders.

Jalen Brewster, Rivals’ No. 1 overall recruit in the 2027 class, has shut down his recruitment and will remain committed to Texas Tech. His family confirmed he will honor his pledge to Joey McGuire’s program over LSU, Florida, Indiana, Ohio State, SMU and Texas.

Brewster considered enrolling early to play this fall in Lubbock but will instead finish his senior season at Buford (Ga.) High.

The 6-foot-3, 300-pound defensive lineman was Rivals’ MVP at the Under Armour All-America Game after his junior season. Rivals scouting director Charles Power called Brewster an “uber-athletic defensive line prospect with true game-wrecking ability” and compared his game to Jalen Carter.

Texas Tech’s class ranks No. 9 nationally and first in the Big 12, according to Rivals. Brewster said: “I feel like Texas Tech is the best place for me and the best place to build my own legacy.”

LSU Abstains as SEC Presidents Approve Penalties for Fielding Returning Pro Athletes

A day after joining a 16-0 vote to enforce the ban, LSU abstained from the vote on sanctions that include a half-season coach suspension and a fine worth 50% of a sport's operating budget.

SEC presidents voted Wednesday to approve penalties for schools that field athletes who dropped out of pro leagues. LSU abstained.

The vote has a direct LSU football connection: Lane Kiffin has recruited at least two players made NCAA eligible by a Louisiana court injunction. LSU men's basketball coach Will Wade was pursuing another such player.

The penalties include a coach suspension for half the season, a fine equal to 50% of that sport's fiscal-year operating budget, loss of conference voting privileges and additional penalties as deemed appropriate. Commissioner Greg Sankey was authorized to enforce the ban with penalties.

LSU had been part of a unanimous 16-0 vote a day earlier to enforce rules against fielding athletes who dropped out of pro leagues.

The injunctions, including the Louisiana injunction, are against the NCAA. The SEC and Big Ten have interpreted them not to cover individual conferences. The Big Ten adopted similar penalties when it voted earlier Tuesday to disallow former pro athletes from returning to rosters.

The NCAA passed an age-based eligibility rule in June that gave athletes a five-year window to play but did not grandfather in Class of 2022 high school athletes who exhausted eligibility under the previous standard.

The players Kiffin recruited have not been identified, and it is unknown whether LSU will field them despite the penalties. Neither Kiffin, Wade nor LSU's administration has publicly commented since Tuesday night's SEC announcement. Lawyers for players said the SEC's rule put the NCAA in contempt, but had not filed suit against the SEC as of Wednesday afternoon.
