Texas Judge Blocks SEC's Pro-Athlete Ban, Putting Two LSU Signees Back in Play

Temporary restraining orders against the NCAA, the SEC and Greg Sankey landed Thursday, days after 15 of 16 SEC schools approved penalties LSU declined to endorse.

A Texas judge granted temporary restraining orders Thursday against the NCAA, the SEC and commissioner Greg Sankey on behalf of a group of former college athletes, freezing enforcement of the conference's new ban on former professional athletes returning to college sports.

The timing matters for LSU more than anyone. The SEC voted unanimously to ban pro athletes from coming back, and on Wednesday 15 of 16 member schools approved the penalty structure attached to it: a head coach suspended for 50% of a season, and a financial penalty equal to 50% of the sport's operating budget. LSU was the lone abstention.

Lane Kiffin has already signed two players the rule was written to keep off the field. Tight end Dae'Quan Wright, formerly of the Cleveland Browns, and defensive tackle Xzavian Harris, formerly of the Saints, are both ex-Ole Miss players now committed to LSU. Kiffin also signed Junior Tuihalamaka, the former Notre Dame defensive end who was not on an NFL roster. On the basketball side, Will Wade added former Big East Player of the Year RJ Luis despite Luis signing contracts with the Utah Jazz and the Celtics' G League affiliate, plus verbal commitments from international pros Márcio Santos, Saliou Niang, Brice Dessert and Michael Ružić.

Wright and Harris are also plaintiffs. The Louisiana eligibility lawsuit, which produced an August 19 temporary restraining order in state court granting more than 30 plaintiffs an additional year, has been amended to name the SEC and Sankey directly. Three former Ole Miss players are part of that suit; two of them are the players LSU signed. Sankey has asked Judge Ronald Johnson in East Baton Rouge Parish court to uphold the NCAA rules preventing athletes from returning.

The Texas order covers a different set of names, including three players currently on NFL rosters: Cowboys receiver Jordan Hudson and Chiefs pair Wesley Bissainthe, a linebacker, and receiver Omari Evans.

Kiffin has been direct about the calculus. "At the end of the day, because of these rulings, they're either going to play for you or they're going to play for someone else around the country," he said. "So we've made the decision to recruit them like probably most people are."

He also framed the conference position as a collision with the courts. "Over 100 athletes in multiple sports were cleared and said you could go play. That's a court order," Kiffin said. "If you think back on all the different court orders of great players that were granted -- whether that's Diego Pavia or, now, Trinidad (Chambliss) that were allowed to play a sixth year, we followed all court orders. I think you get into a really interesting situation as a conference if you're now saying, 'Well, we're not going to follow this court order.'"

Sankey has grounded the rule in eligibility philosophy: "These rules are grounded in the principle that athletics are an integral part of the academic experience. The rules align the time permitted for college athletics with the pursuit of an undergraduate education and further the important purpose of preserving opportunities for future student-athletes to participate in college sports."

Attorney Tony Clayton's argument is that the conference cannot do what the NCAA was barred from doing. "The NCAA, in and of itself, are its member institutions," he said. "You can't camouflage yourself or act as some chameleon and say that you're something else, and you're going to come in and enforce the rule that the judge just said you could not."

What is not settled: how long the Texas order stands, whether the SEC appeals, how Judge Johnson rules in Baton Rouge, and whether LSU can put Wright and Harris on the field in 2026 without triggering a half-season ban for Kiffin. The Big Ten, ACC and Big 12 passed similar penalties and have not been sued yet.

The practical question for LSU, coming off 7-6 and slotted No. 11 in the preseason AP poll: whether two signed players are roster pieces or legal exposure by the time the Tigers open SEC play. They visit Ole Miss on September 19.

Colin Klein Takes Over Kansas State With Former Backup QB Trey Scott as GM

Klein replaces the retired Chris Klieman and pairs with Scott, an 11-year NFL executive and former K-State teammate, after a 6-6 season defined by close losses and new roster holes.

Kansas State's answer to a 6-6 season is Colin Klein at head coach and Trey Scott, his former backup quarterback, as general manager.

Klein took over after Chris Klieman retired at the end of the 2025 season. Scott, who spent 11 years as an executive with the Commanders and Raiders and also worked as a coach's agent, took the general manager job in December 2025. Klein was a Heisman finalist who led Kansas State to No. 2 and a Fiesta Bowl appearance in 2012.

Scott said he had agreed to leave his previous job only if Klein called.

"I promise you, I'm not chasing these jobs. I'm only gonna go if one person calls," Scott said. "And I wrote it down on a piece of paper, and I signed it, and gave it to my wife, and it was Colin [Klein]. Sure as heck, he gets a job."

Scott's specific duties beyond player evaluation have not been detailed.

The Wildcats went 6-6 in 2025, their first non-COVID season with fewer than eight wins since Bill Snyder's final season in 2018. Five of their six losses were one-possession games. They also had one-score wins over FCS opponent North Dakota and Oklahoma State. Klieman's tenure peaked with a 10-win regular season and conference championship in 2022.

Avery Johnson tied last year's results to details Kansas State is emphasizing under Klein.

"At times last year, let the small things kind of slip through the cracks," Johnson said. "I think this year we're kind of trying to hammer down on the small details. No matter how big or how small, that type of stuff translates to wins and losses in the fall."

Johnson said having an offensive-minded head coach gives him someone he can "relate to and spitball ideas off of." Klein spent two years as Texas A&M's offensive coordinator before returning to Manhattan.

The immediate problem is the roster. Leading receiver Jayce Brown transferred to LSU, and Kansas State has not identified his replacement. Offensive linemen John Pastore and George Fitzpatrick suffered season-ending injuries. The passing game struggled last season, and Klein begins without its top receiver and with two offensive-line injuries to address.

Klieman remains with the school in an advisory role and was slowed in the spring by ankle surgery. Klein and Scott now inherit a program that was close in 2025 but enters 2026 with clear questions at receiver and on the offensive line.

Sayin and Moore Head Big Ten Quarterback Tier Ahead of Nov. 7 Meeting

A preseason ranking puts Ohio State's Julian Sayin and Oregon's Dante Moore alone at the top of the league — both with new play-callers, both circling the same November date.

Julian Sayin and Dante Moore sit alone in the top tier of a preseason ranking of Big Ten quarterbacks for 2026. Given that Ohio State opens as the No. 1 team in both the AP and Coaches polls and Oregon as No. 2, the ordering is less a surprise than a reminder of where the November calendar points: Oregon visits Columbus on Nov. 7.

Sayin's 2025 was 32 touchdowns and 3,600 yards, which is the kind of production that survives a preseason list argument. The counterweight is also in the record — losses to Indiana and Miami, with Sayin described as "human" behind a faulty offensive line. That is the part worth tracking, because Ohio State's front is the variable that made a 12-2 season feel less than airtight, and the Buckeyes hit Texas in Week 2 on Sept. 12 before the schedule gives them much runway.

Both headliners are also breaking in a new play-caller. Arthur Smith takes over the Ohio State offense. Moore returns to Oregon — as one of the nation's highest-paid players — to a new coordinator of his own. Continuity at quarterback with turnover above it is the shared condition at the top of the league, and it is the reason September will be more informative than the ranking is.

Below them, the tiers read like a league in transition: Indiana hands the job to Hoover, a former TCU quarterback, replacing Heisman winner Fernando Mendoza; Michigan's Bryce Underwood works under new coordinator Jason Beck; UCLA's Nico Iamaleava plays for first-year coach Bob Chesney after an inconsistent 2025; Maryland is betting Washington can move a program that went 4-8.
