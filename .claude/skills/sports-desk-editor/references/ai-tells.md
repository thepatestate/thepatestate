# The machine tells — what our copy does that the corpus never does

Each tell has a live example from The Pate State (2026-08-28/29) and the fix. Quote the offending sentence when you flag one.

| # | Tell | Live example | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Uniform paragraphs.** Every paragraph is 2–3 sentences of 18–24 words. | Every Wire item on 08-29: lead graf / context graf / schedule graf. | Break one paragraph to a single sentence; let one run five. Merge the schedule into a sentence with a date. |
| 2 | **Stat-stack lead.** The first sentence is a fact plus a number, no tension, no clock. | "Kentucky is listed at +12500 to win the SEC championship and +6000 to reach the SEC Championship Game." | Lead with what happened, when, and what it pushes against (craft-notes §1). |
| 3 | **Self-audit sentence.** The editor's note leaks into the story. | "The reporting does not establish Kiffin's exact compensation or how it is funded." / "It is a Yahoo preseason projection; the Big Ten does not release official preseason projections." | Say who did not say it: "LSU has not disclosed the terms." Or cut. |
| 4 | **Telegraphed contingency pair.** "If A… If it cannot…" | "If A&M can stay on schedule, Reed's passing game becomes harder to defend. If it cannot, the new front will be tested…" | Pick the one that is actually likely and say it as a claim with a number behind it. |
| 5 | **"Will show whether."** Ending by announcing what the future will reveal. | "Arizona State and LSU will show whether the new offensive core is ready. The closing run will show whether the playoff case survives." | Kicker = quote, wry line, or the thesis said harder (craft-notes §10). |
| 6 | **Abstract nouns doing football's job.** ceiling, utility, identity, evidence, resume, outcome, comparison, floor, mechanism, continuity, question, answer. | "Marcel Reed gives A&M a quarterback ceiling most teams cannot manufacture." / "red-zone and big-play utility" / "That has to become September evidence." | Replace with the thing: the throw, the game, the number, the name. |
| 7 | **Stative verbs.** is listed / is projected / remains / enters / is among / gives / provides. | "Kentucky is listed…" "Herbstreit cited…" "Washington remains in…" | passed, banned, named, benched, signed, cut, sued, walked. |
| 8 | **Schedule-graf ending.** The last paragraph is dates. | "…hosting Clemson on Sept. 5 before traveling to Ole Miss on Sept. 19." | One next-thing with a date inside the body; end on a person. |
| 9 | **Quote as proof.** One-sentence quote used to back the preceding sentence. | "Nimrod said Spencer is 'one of the most natural football players I've been around' and praised his route running." | Set the person up in one line, then let them talk for two or three sentences. Use "said." |
| 10 | **Balanced-clause sentences.** "does not eliminate X, but it can keep Y from becoming Z." | "A quarterback who can distribute quickly to a deep receiving corps does not eliminate offensive-line questions, but it can keep every protection issue from becoming a third-and-long issue." | Say the one true half. |
| 11 | **Triplets and lists of games.** Three items, every time; a paragraph that is a schedule. | "Arkansas on Oct. 3, Missouri on Oct. 10 and The Citadel on Oct. 17 before hosting Alabama on Oct. 24." | Two numbers that argue (craft-notes §5); one game that matters. |
| 12 | **No clock, no place.** Nothing tells the reader when or where. | Every item: "is scheduled to open," "enters 2026." | "Tuesday," "four days before kickoff," a dateline if the reporting is local. |
| 13 | **Paragraph-opener adverbs.** Elsewhere, / Meanwhile, / Separately, / Still, / That said, | "Elsewhere, 52% picked Ohio State…" | Cut the adverb; the new paragraph is the transition. |
| 14 | **"X isn't Y. It's Z." and "not because… but because"** | (V2/V3 columns, repeatedly) | Say Z. |
| 15 | **Title Case headline with an abstract noun.** | "Analyst Says Mateer's Decision-Making Issues Predated Thumb Injury" | Sentence case, a verb, the name: "Austin: Mateer was turning down open throws before the thumb injury" |
| 16 | **Hedge stacking.** can / could / may / would have to / is capable of, three to a paragraph. | "11–1 remains a legitimate best-case outcome." | One claim, one hedge at most, with the reason for the hedge. |
| 17 | **Explaining the sport to the fan.** | "A quarterback who can distribute quickly…" | Cut (desk voice rule). |
| 18 | **Announcing the analysis.** "Here's the part that gets missed," "the calendar is doing the work," "the cleaner test is dated." | (Josh's Read drafts) | Make the point instead of pointing at it. |
| 19 | **Every quote from the same source's own article, none from the room.** | Wire items built on one Yahoo/On3 story. | If only one quote exists, use it as a person, once; do not pad with paraphrase of the same quote. |
| 20 | **Off-desk subject.** The piece is competent but no national college football desk would run it. | Dakota Wesleyan at Dakota State; Cass at Northfield (Indiana high school); a reader poll on Purdue; Kentucky's title odds. | Flag it: the fix is upstream (the desk gate), not in the prose. |

## Quick numbers (the script prints these)

- Sentence-length spread: pros' standard deviation is usually > 9 words; machine copy sits at 4–6.
- Paragraph sizes: pros mix 1-sentence and 5-sentence paragraphs; machine copy is all 2–3.
- Time anchors per 100 words (weekday, "this week," "days before," a dated clause): pros ≥ 1; ours ≈ 0.
- Stative-verb share of main verbs: pros < 25%; ours > 50%.
- Abstract-noun hits (list in tell #6): pros ≈ 0–1 per 500 words; ours 4–8.
- Quote share: news 20–40%; reported feature 40–60%; ours 0–10%.
