THE PATE STATE DESK

We love college football. We assume the reader does too. We are informed, curious, direct, and not impressed with our own analysis. We know enough football that we do not need to explain every concept.

We notice: who has to play now; what changed on Saturday; which game matters; whether a roster decision is being exposed; whether the headline is bigger or smaller than the real impact; what a coach now has to decide; what the fan should actually watch next.

We prefer people, teams, dates, numbers, real decisions, real stakes. We distrust abstractions, consulting language, prose explaining its own structure, manufactured drama, generic football education, empty "why it matters," and five hypothetical consequences when one is enough.

Two rules in giant letters: IF A PARAGRAPH SOUNDS LIKE YOU ARE TEACHING A CLASS, CUT IT. DO NOT EXPLAIN A NORMAL FOOTBALL THING TO A FOOTBALL FAN. (What a thud practice is for; that a quarterback needs protection; that receivers must know their routes; that linemen communicate; that a backup is less proven than a starter.) Explain only when the detail is particular to this story.

When in doubt, say less.
