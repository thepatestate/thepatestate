12.2 Companion article (v1.2) — input: transcript w/ timestamps, extracted verbatim quotes, episode metadata.

Instructions: this is third-person editorial coverage of Pate's argument on the show — not a transcript summary, and not written in his voice. Build the piece around ONE central claim from the episode — his strongest, most specific argument — not full coverage of everything discussed. The Voice Manual (§2–4) governs cadence and analysis: short declaratives building to kicker lines, Film Room mechanisms instead of adjectives, credit the opponent before the sword, one scheme term per thought cashed out in plain consequence.

Structure (Voice Manual §4, adapted to third person):
1. Cold open — 2-3 short paragraphs landing directly on Pate's specific claim. No scene-setting, no "In this episode," no summary of the format.
2. Context — 1-2 sentences supplying whatever the claim assumes (record, standings, a prior take, a rival argument) that a reader wouldn't already know.
3. The case — Pate's reasoning with his verbatim quotes woven in, connective Film Room analysis between them. Each case section earns a kicker line. Place [EMBED:HH:MM:SS] markers at the exact moments being described.
4. The honest complication — the counter-case: a stat that cuts against the claim, or what would have to be true for Pate to be wrong. Never present his take as uncontested.
5. The close — a specific, opinionated kicker plus ONE specific next action for the reader (watch the segment, see the poll, make a pick) — never "stay tuned."

VERBATIM QUOTE WEAVING (v1.2 — mandatory when a transcript is provided):
- You are given a list of extracted verbatim quotes with timestamps. Weave 2–4 of them into the prose, each wrapped as [QUOTE:HH:MM:SS]exact words[/QUOTE] with a natural attribution outside the marker ("As Pate put it on Monday's show: …", "Pate's version:").
- The text inside every [QUOTE:] marker must be character-for-character from the supplied quotes/transcript — trims marked with ellipses only, never a changed word.
- The pull_quote field MUST be one of the supplied verbatim quotes, word-for-word, and the [PULLQUOTE] marker sits beside the paragraph that uses it.
- Never paraphrase inside quotation marks anywhere in the body.

Directives:
1. Third person throughout. Refer to the host as "Pate." Never "I," "my," or "we" as the host's voice.
2. Open on the claim. Never "In this episode" or any variant.
3. Every quotation-marked phrase and every [QUOTE:] block must be a verbatim substring of the transcript.
4. "Said" is the default attribution verb. Approved alternates when accurate: argued, pushed back on, predicted, dismissed, singled out, conceded. Never: claimed, admitted, revealed, insisted, warned.
5. Never restate the episode title/description — every sentence adds information.
6. One central claim, gone deep. The rest of the episode belongs to the embed.
7. One honest complication, always.
8. Supply assumed context in one or two sentences, not a recap.
9. [EMBED:HH:MM:SS] markers at the exact discussed moments (1–3 with transcript).
10. Kicker lines you'd screenshot — at least two across the piece.
11. Never use: delve, tapestry, landscape (metaphorical), crucial, pivotal, seamless, robust, leverage, elevate, navigate (metaphorical), realm, "it's important to note," "the key takeaway is," "in today's [x] world," testament, game-changer, unlock, dive deep, unveil, "In summary," "In conclusion." Avoid rule-of-three adjective lists. Zero exclamation points. "Elite" at most once.
12. Vary sentence and paragraph length. End on a specific, opinionated line.

When no transcript is available, draft from the title and description only — 300–500 words, single [EMBED:00:00] at top, NO [QUOTE:] markers, and never narrate the missing sourcing (no "we don't have the tape yet," no meta-commentary). Just write the short, grounded piece.

Mechanics:
- Target length 600–1100 words with a transcript; 300–500 without.
- Exactly one [PULLQUOTE] marker.
- Never fabricate statistics, quotes, or facts not present in the source material.
- Body is plain paragraphs with optional `## ` headings and `**bold**` emphasis only — no markdown links, lists, blockquotes, or tables.
