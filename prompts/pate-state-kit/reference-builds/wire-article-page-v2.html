Five-Star WR Deuce Whitmore Commits to Georgia Over Ohio State and Texas

The No. 2 receiver in the 2027 class picks Athens — the highest-rated pass catcher to commit to Georgia in six cycles, and a direct answer to the one question hanging over the No. 1 team in the JP Poll.

Jamison "Deuce" Whitmore, the No. 2 wide receiver in the 2027 class, announced his commitment to Georgia at his high school this afternoon, choosing the Bulldogs over Ohio State and Texas. He becomes the highest-rated pass catcher to commit to Georgia in six recruiting cycles. Whitmore took official visits to all three finalists this summer and had been trending toward Columbus in most industry projections as recently as July. His announcement is on the record and on video; this one is not a projection.

Why It Matters

  • Georgia's roster has exactly one publicly acknowledged soft spot — the receiver room, the boldest personnel bet any contender made this offseason — and the program just answered it at the source: recruiting, where its pipeline never actually broke.
  • Whitmore is a contested-catch outside receiver, the specific archetype the current depth chart lacks. This is a need-matched commitment, not a rankings trophy.
  • Two rivals lost the battle at once: Ohio State, which led most projections for two months, and Texas, which hosted the final visit.

The Pate State Read · Desk analysis — Josh has not yet commented on today's news

Credit the recruitment itself first: beating Ohio State for an elite receiver, in this era, with Columbus producing the position's best recent résumés, is a genuine board win — not a formality of geography. The honest caveat is the calendar. Whitmore doesn't catch a pass for Georgia until 2027, and the 2026 receiver question this commitment rhymes with remains exactly as open as it was this morning. What today actually changes is the pressure map: the program just demonstrated the room's talent pipeline is fine, which moves the burden of proof squarely onto development — the one thing this staff has never needed to outsource. A decommitment is always possible between now and signing day; that risk is real and it is priced in below.
